LabelLoop Replication Guide

How to rebuild the simulated parcel-sorting cell on the real X Square desktop arms: where everything goes, what to print, how the harness decides, and what to calibrate on the day.

Sim layout: cases 18–23 ("row") Harness: pick-and-place, 3 tries Home pose: confirm on robot

What the demo does

A person slides a parcel by hand into a marked zone in front of the robot, standing in for a conveyor. LabelLoop waits until the parcel stops moving and the hand is gone, then reads its QR label. The label carries a ship-to ZIP code, and LabelLoop routes on it the way a parcel hub does. ZIPs in lane A go to the blue bin and ZIPs in lane B to the orange bin. An unknown ZIP, a missing label, or something that isn't a parcel goes to the black reject bin for inspection. LabelLoop tells the WALLX model what to do one sentence at a time, checks the result with the camera, retries up to three times, and then waits for the next parcel.

The robot picks and places; it does not swipe. In simulation, WALLX never pushed a parcel (0 of 20 episodes), never acted on flat target mats, and never reached a basket outside the camera view (0 of 8). Open bins on the table are the only targets it has hit.

Props to print and build

Parcels

60 mm cubes in kraft brown, so the harness can tell a parcel from garbage. The G gripper opens about 97 mm. Put a 40 mm QR code with a 5 mm white quiet zone on the top face and one side face. Run sim/make_qr_boxes.py to print the A4 label sheet; it decodes every label to check it before saving.

Garbage and no-label

Garbage is any non-kraft object; sim used a red 50 mm cube. For the no-label case, use a plain kraft cube with no code on it.

Sort plan

The payload follows GS1 Application Identifier 420, the ship-to postal code, as 420/<ZIP5>/<seq3>. Like a hub's sortation computer, the harness routes on the 3-digit ZIP prefix. Anything without a route goes to the exception lane. The plan lives in configs/task_qr_sort.yaml.

System

State machine

Exact sentences sent to WALLX

StateInstructionWhen
PLACEput the block into the blue binlane A (orange for lane B)
REJECTput the block into the black binunknown ZIP, bad code, no label
REJECTput the object into the black bingarbage (not kraft)
HOMEput both arms back to the home positionafter every attempt

Keep the wording short and free of location phrases. "put the block into the blue bin" is the only phrasing that scored zero-shot. "push", "move … onto the mat", and "drop it off the right side" all scored 0.

Event-day checklist

  1. Lay out the cell. Measure the robot origin, then tape the bin positions and the induction zone using the placement table. Keep every bin on the workbench; the rules confine demos to the organizer's workspace.
  2. Read the real home pose. With the arms at home, record one EndPose from the WebUI and put it in configs/ex001_6r.yaml → home_pose. Sim assumes (0.1185, 0, 0.181) m in each arm's frame. The API guide's example state is around (0.29, ±0.03, 0.38), so expect this number to change. Check it with the round trip: send the current pose back as the target, and the arm must not move.
  3. Check the gripper range. The real G gripper is 0–1.526 (API guide) and sim is 0–1.89. The harness maps linearly between them.
  4. Check camera sides. The API guide says the right arm appears on the left of the image, while in sim the left arm is on the left. Take a head-camera frame and note which arm is where. The bins stay where they are relative to the robot. Re-measure the ROI from the real image.
  5. Calibrate perception on venue light. Grab frames of the empty cell and of a parcel in the zone. Set scene.induction_roi as image fractions (sim: 0.46, 0.48, 0.78, 0.92). Then check the blue, orange and kraft HSV ranges in configs/task_qr_sort.yaml and confirm each bin's colour box is detected.
  6. Print and test the labels. Each label must decode from the wrist camera at 10–20 cm in at least 9 of 10 frames, the same gate as in sim.
  7. Run the five single-parcel cases (A, B, unknown ZIP, no label, garbage), then a five-parcel stream, spacing parcels so each finishes before the next arrives.
  8. Start the relay: .venv/bin/python -m labelloop.robots.event_server --host <laptop LAN IP> --port 8001. Then in the WebUI set the model address to ws://<laptop LAN IP>:8001, EndPose, sync. Stop the robot from the WebUI, never by killing the relay.
