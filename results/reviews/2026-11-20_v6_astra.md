### Blockers

1. **The planned “contact” can be in free space.** `look.py:84–94,216–231` (`footprint`, `spin_plan`); `harness.py:117–127`. `hw` is half a projected **diagonal**, not the distance to a face along `d`; `d` points toward the arm, not necessarily along a face normal. Even for a perfectly localized 120 mm square approached face-on, the ideal half-diagonal is 85 mm: the nominal contact is 25 mm outside the face, and the entire tangential stroke stays outside it. Missing segmentation substitutes 120 mm rather than refusing. Fit an oriented footprint against the green mat, select an actual edge, account for finger-pad geometry, and refuse a push if the edge is uncertain. `tests/test_v6.py`’s `run_spin` calls `sc.push()` on waypoint **index**, without executing a contact.

2. **Checked trajectory ≠ commanded trajectory.** `look.py:189–213` (`reach`, `path`); `wallx_client.py:106–125` (`move_response`); `harness.py:130–160,189–209`. IK checks interpolated **tip** positions, discards intermediate solutions, then the client interpolates **EE** position and orientation from measured state. Home→approach, retreat→home and calibration transits are not checked. No link, mat, bin or other-arm collision test exists; the clearance predicate uses the *requested* tip, not achieved FK. A feasible endpoint does not make calibration fail-closed. Validate the actual 32 targets from current state, including returns, using continuous joint solutions and swept geometry; stop on tracking error before advancing.

3. **Calibration is not isolated.** `build_task.py:52–63`; `harness.py:35–41,130–162`. Stock cases inject a moving parcel at 6 sim seconds, about three chunks into calibration. After the schedule, even if every point was refused, the harness falls through to normal sorting. Use an empty, event-free calibration case and latch DONE/hold. Compare rendered fingertip pixels and independent mat fiducials to predictions: `measured_tip` reuses the very base transform under test.

4. **The eight-minute deadline is not enforced.** `state_machine.py:35–38,102–116` (`_fresh`, `_done`, `step`); `look.py:51–69`; `build_task.py:55–63`. Spending resets per parcel, ignores IDLE and synchronous planning, and uses fixed cloud latency. IK takes all 60 finite-difference iterations even at convergence within a seed. Separately, four full spins consume ~85 sim seconds (40 chunks) before delivery in a 150-s single-parcel case. Use a monotonic **case** deadline plus remaining sim steps, charge planning/network time, and admit a search try only if its full cost leaves delivery and home time.

### Should-fix

5. **A visible valid QR can still lose to garbage.** `harness.py:53–74`; `state_machine.py:125–139` (SCAN). The last IDLE observation clears confirmation history. On the first SCAN image a good raw decode has count one; low kraft immediately routes to reject before count two. Defer garbage until the confirmation window expires. Also associate wrist decodes with the current object, not merely the same camera twice; currently even SCAN accepts unrestricted wrist images.

6. **Retries need not expose new faces.** `look.py:217–232`; `state_machine.py:65–80`; `harness.py:66–72`. Alternating `s` reverses torque, generally with the same nearest arm; the second stroke can undo the first. Bottom labels cannot be exposed by yaw at all. Also, enabled LOOK reached **from SPIN** is never planned (`plan_look` requires `before == "SCAN"`). Track achieved orientation, continue toward unseen faces, plan on every LOOK entry, and test a separate flip or explicitly budget for bottom-face rejects.

7. **Area is not grasp width.** `state_machine.py:125–130`; `task_qr_sort.yaml` (`big_presence`). Perspective or a long narrow box can trigger LIFT2 and skip search; a shorter but too-wide parcel can get an impossible one-arm grasp. Compare fitted narrow dimension plus clearance to 97.5 mm, and check grasp/bin reach for the assigned arm.

**Verdict:** **(a)** Yes to a short, isolated, supervised sim calibration after disabling injection and stopping at schedule end. **(b)** No to hours on this spin; run an instrumented single-contact probe after fixing edge localization and transit checks. **(c)** No to 100 episodes yet.

**Push geometry:** 55 mm root z is **40 mm above the nominal table**, less above a raised mat. Mat friction alone predicts neither yaw nor tipping: tangential traction needs inward preload. For a centered load pushed horizontally, tipping competes with sliding when \(\mu h\gtrsim b\), with \(b\) the support half-width along the force: 38 mm is marginal at \(h=40\) mm and \(\mu=1\). Use a slow **inward, face-normal push near one end** of a measured edge, low on the sidewall with verified mat clearance. That produces yaw torque without relying on tangential grip, though translation remains. Use short strokes; measure contact, yaw and centroid displacement, retract and re-localize before continuing.
