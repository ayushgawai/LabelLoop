## Ranked findings

### BLOCKER

**1. SPIN-try counter is never reset on the SCAN→SPIN loop; tries leak across search cycles.**
`state_machine.py:_fresh()` resets `self.tries = {}` only on entry to IDLE. When SPIN exhausts and `_search_next` routes back to SCAN (because SPIN found no code but there are still tries left), the SPIN counter persists. If the next SCAN call also fails, it transitions to SPIN again with `self.tries["SPIN"]` already at, say, 3 of 4. After one more failure it becomes 4, the next SPIN attempt skips immediately — the parcel gets rejected without using all its legitimate SPIN attempts. The same leak applies to LOOK (`self.looked` is correctly reset in `_fresh`, but the try counter for any future state would leak the same way).  
*Fix:* Move `self.tries = {}` into `_fresh()` (already there) but also reset it on every entry to SCAN, or gate `_search_next`'s SPIN branch on a per-cycle counter.

**2. Budget does not bound the SPIN push path cost; a long push can silently overrun the deliver reserve.**
In `harness.py:step`, `push = len(self.spin["wps"]) + self.cfg["home_calls"]` is computed, but `self.sm.step(...)` is called with `"spin_push": push` and `"spin_calls": push + sc`. The state machine uses these to end the SPIN state, but **`self.spent` is never updated during SPIN** — `_cost(s)` for state `"SPIN"` falls into the `scripted_call_s` branch, and `step()` adds `_cost(s)` to `spent` at the top. So `spent` *does* accumulate during SPIN. However, `_search_next` only checks `remaining() < deliver_reserve_s` at the moment of transition. After SPIN finishes and the head camera re-reads, if the re-read SCAN calls exhaust `scan_max_calls` (6 × 2.2 = 13.2 s), the state machine transitions to the *next* search step (LOOK or INSPECT) without re-checking the budget until that step's own `_search_next`. A near-budget case can therefore silently consume the deliver reserve before the first delivery attempt.  
*Fix:* Add `if self.remaining() < self.cfg["budget"]["deliver_reserve_s"]: self._route(None, "budget")` at the top of `step()` whenever `s != "IDLE"` and a WALLX call is about to be made (i.e., in `PLACE`/`REJECT`/`LIFT2`/`INSPECT` entry).

### SHOULD-FIX

**3. Wrist view azimuth range silently discards the far-side of the near half-circle, making SPIN the only reliable path for parcels near one arm's home.**
`look.py:views` ranges `deg = 90..270` (15° steps). `_view` checks IK feasibility. The left arm's IK envelope from the URDF chain reaches roughly 90°–195° azimuth; the right arm roughly 15°–120°. The union covers 15°–195° — a 180° gap from 195° to 15° through the far side. For a parcel at `y = +0.12` m (right of centre), the left arm covers 90°–150° and the right arm covers roughly 60°–105°; the overlap zone is 90°–105°. If `sep_deg = 60°` and the greedy selector picks 180° first (closest to 180°), no arm reaches 180° from that y-position — `views()` returns `[]` with no warning. The harness then holds in LOOK for `look_calls` and falls through to `_search_next`, consuming budget for nothing.  
*Fix:* Either warn in the runlog when `views()` returns `[]` (so the operator knows LOOK is dead for that geometry), or widen the azimuth range to 30°–330° and let `_view`'s IK gate do the honest work.

**4. `spin_plan` tries pitches 90°→75°→60°→45° and returns the first that clears IK. Lower pitch = fingertip points more horizontally = larger moment arm for yawing, but also higher risk of the box toppling over.**
For a 76 mm cube at zt ≈ 0.025 m fingertip height, pushing horizontally (45° pitch) at the top of the box (0.055 m) gives a toppling torque arm of 0.055−0.038 = 17 mm. The static toppling threshold for a 1.0-μ box is roughly `(hw/2) / h_box ≈ 38/80 ≈ 0.47`. The push force needed to topple is ~3× the force needed to slide it — but the push is tangential, so the box will yaw *and* slide. The current clearance check only verifies fingertip-to-box distance and fingertip height; it does not check whether the push geometry risks toppling. For the small 76×76×50 mm parcel, a horizontal push at the top third is more likely to topple than to yaw in place.  
*Fix:* Bound the pitch based on the estimated box CoM: `pitch_max = min(75°, arctan((hw−clearance)/hz))` or add a topple-stability check. Alternatively, push at the lower third of the box height (tip z ≈ table + 0.01 m) to maximise yaw torque and minimise topple risk.

**5. `IK` solver's 60-iteration inner loop uses a fixed `1e-4` damping and a hard 60-step limit with no residual plateau check. For joint-limit-heavy configurations near the edge of the workspace, the solver may converge to a local minimum with `e > 1e-3` that still passes the greedy "best of 8" check, returning a pose whose fingertip is 5–10 mm off the commanded waypoint.**
The `path` function already checks `r[2]` (the joint angles) against LO/HI with `1e-6` tolerance, but `_view`'s clearance check uses `tip[2] - TABLE_Z < cfg["finger_clear"]` which only guards against table collision — not against a fingertip that clips the parcel side because IK converged poorly. For the push path, a 10 mm fingertip deviation at contact means the push may miss the parcel entirely.  
*Fix:* After the IK loop, if `e > 0.005`, do one Gauss-Newton step with the actual (non-damped) Jacobian at the best seed before declaring failure, or raise the damping to `1e-3` for better convergence at the cost of more iterations.

### MINOR

**6. `harness.py:step` recomputes `bins = [scene.roi_box(...) for c in cfg["bin_rois"]]` on every call inside the `if before in ("LOOK", ...)` branch, even though `cfg["bin_rois"]` and `cfg["scene"]` are constant within an episode.**
Negligible cost per call, but `scene.roi_box` calls `cfg["scene"]["bin_rois"]` and does array math; in a 32-frame WALLX chunk this is called 32×. Cache the bin ROI boxes at episode start.  
*Fix:* Compute `self._bin_rois = [scene.roi_box(np.zeros((720,1280)), cfg, "bin_rois", c) for c in cfg["bin_rois"]]` in `reset()`.

**7. The wrist camera optical-axis direction in `_view` uses `CAM_OFF = [0.074, 0, 0.098]` as an EE→camera offset, but the brief says "optical axis = camera_base +x". The camera optical axis in the gripper frame is indeed +x after the rpy `(0, 0.436, 0)` rotation. The code computes `ee = cam - R @ CAM_OFF - frame(arm)`, which places the EE such that `ee + R @ CAM_OFF = cam` (correct: camera is at `ee + R·CAM_OFF`). However, the `aim_down` logic adds the tilt *after* the arctan2 of the look direction, not as a rotation applied to the optical axis. For `aim_down = 0.4 rad ≈ 23°` and `CAM_TILT = 0.436 rad ≈ 25°`, the total pitch is `th - 0.436`. If `th ≈ 0` (camera roughly horizontal), the optical axis pitches to −25°, which looks *downward* — correct for a downward-looking wrist cam. But the label land in the upper image half" intent depends on the decode tolerance; the current aim-down pushes the label to the top ~40% of the frame, which is fine.**
This is correct behaviour, just noting it for the record: the implementer's claim of consistency holds.

**8. `spin_plan` returns `None` if *no* arm/pitch combination works, but `harness.py:step` passes `"spin_push": 0` and `"spin_calls": 1` to the state machine when `self.spin is None`, making the SPIN state skip itself in one call. This is correct, but the state machine then immediately calls `_search_next`, which may select LOOK or INSPECT without re-evaluating whether the parcel is now reachable from a different position (e.g., it shifted during the failed SPIN attempt).**  
*Fix:* After a failed SPIN (no feasible push), call `plan_spin` again with the new parcel centroid before transitioning to LOOK/INSPECT.

## Push-geometry question

A tangential push at fingertip height ≈ 55 mm on a 76–229 mm box on friction-1.0 mat: **it will mostly translate, not yaw.**

The torque from a 0.07 m tangential push at 50% of the box width (offset ≈ 19–57 mm) is τ = F × 0.019–0.057 m. For a 70–90 g cardboard box, the rotational inertia about the vertical axis is I ≈ ⅙ m (w² + d²) ≈ 4–20 × 10⁻⁴ kg·m². The yaw angular acceleration is τ/I ≈ 50–500 rad/s² — which *sounds* fast, but the Coulomb friction torque τ_fric = μ × m × g × (w/2) ≈ 0.5 × 0.08 × 9.81 × 0.04 ≈ 16 mN·m dominates the applied torque for all but the largest box. The box will slide sideways (translate) before it yaws appreciably.

**Better geometry:** push at the *top edge* ( fingertip z ≈ table + 0.10 m for a 76 mm box, or at the top third for the 229 mm box) with the fingertip *above* the CoM, pushing *horizontally* (pitch ≈ 45°) at an angular offset of ~15–20° off the radial line — this maximises the moment arm about the vertical axis while keeping the horizontal component at the contact point small enough that the box slides less than it rotates. For the 76 mm box, push at z ≈ 0.06 m (just above the top face), pitch 60°, offset 0.25 × hw; for the 229 mm box, z ≈ 0.12 m, pitch 50°, offset 0.3 × hw. In code terms: set `push_pitch = min(75°, 90° - 5°)` and `zt = TABLE_Z + min(parcel_h * 0.85, 0.10)` so the contact point is in the upper third of the box.
